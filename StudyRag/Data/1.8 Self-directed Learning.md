# 1.8 Self-directed Learning

Self-directed learning is all about taking charge of your own learning—setting goals, finding resources, preparing for assessments, and diving into the material to really make it stick. As a support worker, building this skill is key to handling challenges, improving your practice, and growing professionally.

Each week, you’ll have self-directed learning activities to go along with your online learning. These are part of your allocated hours and a great way to revisit, build on, and expand what you’ve learned so far. Remember, these activities aren’t limited to what’s provided—you can include other learning opportunities that work for you, like reading, research, or discussions with peers.

So, make the most of it! Approach these activities with curiosity and an open mind. They’re here to help you connect your learning to real-world situations and grow your confidence in your role.

**Week 1:**

- Write a letter of introduction to your Tutor, include a bit about yourself and the reason you have chosen to study healthcare. This will be confidential to your tutor; you can email the letter or hand in to the tutor in the morning before class.
- Organise your notes and learning resources by setting up a system that works best for you. Choose a method that makes it quick and easy to locate topics when it’s time for revision.
- If you have not done so already, watch episode 1 from the  docudrama- What Really Happened – Waitangi. You can access it by clicking [HERE](https://www.nzonscreen.com/title/waitangi-what-really-happened-2011).
- At the end of this week, review what you've learned both online and in class. Write down any questions to discuss in your next class session.

**Week 2:**

- Click [HERE](https://teara.govt.nz/en/primary-health-care) to go to the Te Ara story of Primary Health Care. Explore the complete story on pages 1-5 to add to your learning from this week.
- Hauora is a Māori term that encompasses the holistic approach to health and wellbeing. Do your own research into Hauora and its four dimensions to add to your online and class learning. Then write a short summary to explain the key parts.

**Week 3:**

- Work on your  assessment
- When working as a support worker in health care you need to be aware of the person’s rights you are caring for. Write up a list of the rights of patients that you need to remember.
- Think about the tasks and duties of a support worker that you've explored in your online learning. Take some time to reflect:
  - What knowledge and experience do you already have?
  - What skills and knowledge do you need to develop further?
- Think about the New Zealand’s health and wellbeing sector aims that we have looked at. These are:
  - Equitable Health Outcomes
  - Improving Health Outcomes
  - Voice at the Heart of the System
  - Person- and Whānau-Centred Care
  - Integrated Care
  - Preventative Care and Health Promotion
  - A Learning Culture

For each of the aims, write down the following:

- Describe in your own words what the aim means.
- Reflect on how achieving this aim could positively impact the health and wellbeing of individuals, families, and communities.

**Week 4:**

- Work on your  assessment.
- Over the next week, make a point of observing how people communicate with each other in everyday settings (e.g., at the supermarket, in the park, or during family conversations). Pay attention to the following aspects:
  - Active listening: How do people show they are listening and engaged?
  - Non-verbal cues: What body language, facial expressions, or gestures do people use to support their communication?
  - Empathy and respect: How do people express empathy or respect in their conversations?
  - Clarity: How do people ensure their message is understood?

**Reflection:** After observing, reflect on what communication techniques stood out to you. How can you use similar approaches to engage effectively with tangata and whānau in a health and wellbeing setting? Write a summary of your thoughts and ideas.

**Week 5:**

- In your own words, describe at least key factors that contribute to a person’s vulnerability. Consider how these factors may interact with each other, making someone more susceptible to challenges or risks.
- Review and describe in your own words the importance of personal boundaries in support work.
- In your own words, describe what signs of neglect or abuse a support worker should be aware of and why it’s important to recognise them early.

**Week 6:**

You’ve made it through the Introduction to the Aotearoa NZ Health and Wellbeing Sector module—great job! Now, it’s time to reflect and get everything in order for the next step.

- Reflect on what you’ve learned:
  - Take a moment to think about the key points and concepts from this module. What stood out to you?
  - What new knowledge do you feel is most important for your role in health and wellbeing?
- Organise your notes and resources:
  - Go through the notes and resources you’ve gathered. Organise them in a way that makes sense to you—whether that’s by topic, importance, or any other method that helps you stay on top of things. Don’t forget to highlight or tag any sections that you might need to revisit later on!
- Reflect on your study skills
  - Think about how organising your learning will help you in future modules and as a support worker.
  - How does it feel to have your notes in order and a clear understanding of where you’ve been and where you’re headed?
