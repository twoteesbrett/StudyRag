Photosynthesis is the process by which plants use light energy to make sugars from carbon dioxide and water. Chlorophyll absorbs light, and oxygen is released as a by-product. The sugars provide energy and building material for plant growth.

The water cycle describes how water moves between the atmosphere, land, and bodies of water. Evaporation turns liquid water into water vapour. Condensation forms cloud droplets, and precipitation returns water to the surface as rain or snow.

Honey bees communicate the location of food sources using a waggle dance. The direction of the dance indicates the direction of the food relative to the Sun. The duration of the waggle portion provides information about the distance to the food.

Volcanoes form where molten rock reaches the Earth's surface. Magma is molten rock below the surface, while lava is molten rock that has emerged above it. Repeated eruptions can build layers of rock around a volcanic vent.

Sound travels as vibrations through a medium such as air, water, or a solid. Sound cannot travel through a vacuum because there are no particles to transmit the vibrations. Frequency affects the pitch of a sound, while amplitude relates to its loudness.

Composting breaks down organic material such as vegetable scraps and dry leaves. Microorganisms need moisture and oxygen to decompose the material effectively. Turning a compost pile introduces air and helps mix its contents.
